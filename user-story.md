1. Lead capture and customer onboarding
A facilities manager or homeowner visits the PHI‑DPS customer portal and requests service for a fault (e.g., a heating system breakdown). If it’s their first time, they complete a short registration form; returning clients simply log in. The portal collects contact details, property type, preferred time slots, a brief description of the issue and any photos or supporting documents.
Behind the scenes, the CRM module captures the enquiry as a Lead. If the client already exists, the request is attached to their existing Customer record; otherwise, a new customer profile is created. Automated email/SMS confirmations acknowledge receipt and set expectations for response times.
2. Quoting and estimation
The dispatcher reviews the lead in the CRM and assesses whether a site visit is needed. For straightforward jobs, they generate a quote directly by selecting the service type (e.g., boiler repair, CP12 inspection) and adding labour rates, materials and travel costs. For larger commercial jobs or plant room installations, the dispatcher may schedule an initial survey visit, create a preliminary scope and then prepare a formal quote.
The Quoting & Estimation module calculates totals, applies pricing rules and produces a professional PDF. The quote is sent to the client via the portal and email. The client can review, ask questions, and accept or decline digitally. Acceptance triggers a deposit request if required and automatically converts the quote into a confirmed Job.
3. Job creation and scheduling
Once the quote is accepted, the system creates a job record with the customer’s details, location, scope of work, required certifications (e.g., CP12, CP17 or EICR), assets involved and estimated duration. The Inventory & Parts module reserves any parts needed from warehouse or van stock.
The Dispatch module matches the job to the best‑suited engineer based on skill set (Gas Safe, F‑Gas, electrical), availability, current location (from GPS), equipment on board, and workload. The route optimisation engine considers other scheduled jobs to minimise travel time. A job assignment is created, and a notification is pushed to the engineer’s mobile app with the job details and scheduled time.
4. Pre‑visit preparation
The assigned engineer logs into the Mobile Engineer app to view upcoming jobs. They check the job description, review asset history, see any special access requirements, and confirm that all necessary parts/tools are in the van. The fleet tracking module verifies that the vehicle is healthy and fuelled. Before heading to site, the engineer clocks in via the app; the Time Tracking & Payroll module records the start time and GPS location, ensuring accurate timesheets.
5. Travel and arrival
The engineer uses the app’s navigation (integrated with the dispatch module) to travel to the customer’s location. The Fleet & GPS Tracking service continuously sends vehicle positions; dispatchers can monitor progress, and the system can provide customers with estimated arrival times. Upon arrival, geofencing triggers an “On Site” status update, and the engineer officially starts the job timer.
6. On‑site work and documentation
During the visit, the engineer carries out the required tasks—diagnosing faults, repairing equipment, installing new components or performing inspections. Using the mobile app, they:
Capture photos of equipment before and after work.
Record measurements, readings and test results.
Update asset records and note any anomalies for predictive maintenance.
Complete digital checklists for safety and quality.
Consume parts and update inventory counts in real time.
If the work involves regulatory compliance (e.g., a landlord gas safety check, boiler service or commercial installation), the Compliance & Certification module auto‑generates the appropriate certificate. The engineer enters required data (serial numbers, flue readings, combustion analysis), and the system produces a signed CP12, CP17, CP42 or EICR document. Digital signatures from both engineer and client are captured on the device, and the certificate is stored securely.
7. Job completion and handover
When the work is finished, the engineer walks the client through what was done, provides maintenance advice if needed and answers any questions. They mark the job as completed in the app, capture the client’s signature, and optionally collect payment on site (if card processing is enabled). The engineer clocks out; the time‑tracking module records the end time and calculates total hours. If follow‑up work or parts orders are needed, the engineer raises them immediately.
8. Invoicing and payment
Completion triggers the Invoicing & Payments service. It compiles labour time (from timesheets), materials used (from inventory records) and any surcharges, then generates an itemised invoice. The invoice, along with any compliance certificates and job report, is sent to the customer via the portal and email. Customers can pay online through integrated gateways, and once payment clears, the accounting system is updated.
9. Payroll and subcontractor settlement
The Payroll module aggregates hours worked from clock‑in/out records (validated against GPS data) and applies overtime rules, call‑out premiums or bonuses. It generates payroll reports for in‑house engineers and prepares payment instructions for subcontractors. All time and attendance data flows seamlessly into the company’s HR or payroll system.
10. Post‑job analytics and maintenance planning
All data—job duration, parts used, asset condition, certificate status, and customer feedback—is fed into the Analytics & Reporting module. Managers view dashboards showing job completion times, engineer utilisation, revenue per service type, compliance status and asset performance. Predictive maintenance algorithms analyse asset histories to forecast when plant rooms or boilers will need servicing next, generating proactive work orders.
11. Continuous customer engagement
Customers can log into the portal at any time to see service history, download certificates, request new work or renew maintenance contracts. Automated reminders alert them when certificates are due for renewal or assets require servicing. Marketing emails or SMS campaigns (managed through the CRM) can promote additional services or offer contract renewals.
